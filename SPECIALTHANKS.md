# Special Thanks 🎬

The tradition of dedication is as old as oral tradition itself, but was given a different providence when Special Thanks credits began to appear in films, documentaries, and movies. This tradition was kept apace by video games, who had to fight for their right to be credited, in a world where this was commonplace for films since Star Wars began including everyone. We keep it now, in this software, to remember that history, and to give that thanks.

This project exists because of inspiration, tools, and people who made it possible.

## 🎮 Creative Inspiration

**Nintendo Power Magazine** — For 13 years of my childhood (1990-2003), you were the window to worlds I couldn't afford to visit. Issue No. 50 (July 1993) featuring Link's Awakening became the visual foundation for this entire site. Thank you for showing me that games were more than entertainment—they were art, community, and imagination made tangible.

**John Carmack** — Your `.plan` files in the 1990s pioneered the idea of "working in public" before that phrase existed. The transparency, technical depth, and humanity in those updates shaped how I think about development logs.

**Derek Sivers** — For starting the `/now` page movement and showing that personal websites can be both simple and profound. Your philosophy of "ideas not yet proven right" gave me permission to publish work-in-progress.

## 🛠️ Technical Foundation

**Zach Leatherman** — For creating Eleventy, which proves static site generators can be fast, flexible, and fun. Your commitment to simplicity and performance makes building for the web joyful.

**Jeremy Thomas** — For Bulma CSS, which strikes the perfect balance between power and simplicity. The "just add classes" philosophy makes prototyping feel like magic.

**The WebGL/GLSL Community** — For thousands of ShaderToy experiments, tutorials, and Stack Overflow answers that taught me fragment shaders. Special thanks to those who explained concepts like signed distance fields, noise functions, and raymarching with patience and clarity.

**Google Fonts Team** — For making typography accessible to everyone. Coral Pixels, Spectral, Sixtyfour Convergence, and Atkinson Hyperlegible turned this site from good to distinctive.

## 🧠 Mental Health & Peer Support

**Iowa Fairweather Lodge** — For showing me what recovery looks like when we build it together. The Fairweather model proved that peers supporting peers isn't just effective—it's transformative.

**Shelter House Iowa** — For trusting me to bring peer support to emergency shelter work, and for understanding that people experiencing homelessness need allies, not saviors.

**The Peer Support Specialists I've Worked With** — You taught me that lived experience is expertise, that vulnerability is strength, and that the best help comes from people who've been there.

## 🌐 Open Source Heroes

**The Nix/NixOS Community** — For building a package manager that makes "it works on my machine" obsolete. Your commitment to reproducibility changed how I think about infrastructure.

**The Linux Desktop Community** — For 20+ years of proving that computing freedom matters. Wayland, Hyprland, and the endless experimentation keep the dream alive.

**Stack Overflow Contributors** — For answering millions of questions with patience, precision, and occasional snark. You are the unsung heroes of every developer's journey.

## 📚 Writers & Teachers

**Andy Hunt & Dave Thomas** (_The Pragmatic Programmer_) — For teaching that code is craft, and craft requires care. "Good enough software" isn't about lowering standards—it's about knowing when to ship.

**Robin Sloan** (_An App Can Be a Home-Cooked Meal_) — For articulating why small, personal software matters. Your essay gave me language for why I build things "just for me" and share them anyway.

**Maggie Appleton** — For visual explanations that make complex ideas delightful. Your digital garden philosophy showed me that websites can grow organically.

## 💡 Communities & Spaces

**The IndieWeb Movement** — For insisting that we can own our content, control our identities, and connect without corporate gatekeepers. You keep the original web dream alive.

**The Iowa Tech Community** — For being small enough to feel like neighbors and big enough to learn from. Cedar Rapids, Iowa City, and the Corridor cities punch above their weight.

**GitHub** — For hosting this site, providing CI/CD, and making collaboration frictionless. The modern web wouldn't exist without you.

## 🎨 Design Philosophy

**Edward Tufte** — For teaching that data visualization is about clarity, not decoration. "Above all else show the data" applies to web design too.

**Dieter Rams** — For "less but better." The 10 principles of good design apply to code as much as products.

**The Brutalist Web Design Movement** — For reminding us that raw, honest, fast websites are beautiful. Accessibility and performance aren't compromises—they're features.

## 🌟 Personal Acknowledgments

**To everyone who said "you should build that"** — Your encouragement turned ideas into real things. Sometimes all we need is someone to say "yes, do the thing."

**To people who use this site as a learning resource** — You're the reason I document everything. Knowing this helps someone makes the extra effort worthwhile.

**To past me, circa 1997** — You spent hours viewing source on every webpage, reading HTML guides printed from library computers, and building terrible GeoCities sites. This one's for you, kid. You made it.

## 🔮 Influences Beyond the Screen

**The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening** — For teaching me that games can be surreal, melancholic, and beautiful. The Wind Fish's dream still haunts me 31 years later.

**Chrono Trigger** — For showing that time travel stories can have heart. The Time Gate shader on the /now page is my small tribute.

**Super Metroid** — For teaching that exploration requires both courage and tools. You can't go everywhere immediately, and that's the point.

## 🙏 Final Thanks

To **you**, reading this page — thank you for caring enough to get this far. In a world of infinite content, your attention matters. Whether you're here to learn, fork this project, or just see what someone in Iowa is building, I'm grateful you stopped by.

This site is a love letter to the early web, retro gaming, peer support values, and the belief that we can build things that are both useful and beautiful.
